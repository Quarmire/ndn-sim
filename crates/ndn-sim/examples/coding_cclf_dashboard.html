ndn-sim · comparative eval #68

Coding × CCLF: a tidy hypothesis that didn't survive measurement

The expected story was a synergy: CCLF suppresses redundant rebroadcasts, coding makes every surviving one any-K-of-N innovative, so RLNC + CCLF should be the sweet spot for disseminating a generation across a field. It isn't. On a real GF(2)-basis model (fixed across four debugging passes) the truth is blunter: the two axes aren't the same kind of lever. CCLF is the win — it halves airtime at scale. Coding is ~neutral for whole-network flooding, because flooding already gives every packet massive spatial redundancy. Coding's real payoff is a different problem entirely (the lossy relay path). Keeping the negative result is the point.

A delivery vs loss · N=120
Coding buys no robustness here

All four configs track together as per-link erasure climbs to 50%. There's no coupon wall for plain to hit — flood redundancy means every packet arrives by many paths. The any-K-of-N property has nothing to repair.

B airtime vs density · e=0.2
CCLF is the whole story

Flood's transmissions blow up with density — the broadcast storm. CCLF stays flat by overhearing that neighbours already covered the content. Coding barely shifts either curve.

C efficiency · N=120, e=0.3
The 3% that isn't a sweet spot

Delivered nodes per 1000 transmissions. CCLF lifts efficiency ~25%; adding coding on top of CCLF moves it another ~3% — inside the margin. No synergy.

D the model-debugging trail
How a wrong-direction result got fixed

The first runs showed coding worse under loss — backwards. Chasing that (not forcing the narrative) is what produced the honest answer.

Lesson (again): a wrong-direction result means the harness is wrong, not the world. Four fixes later, the world's answer was simply "coding doesn't help this workload."
