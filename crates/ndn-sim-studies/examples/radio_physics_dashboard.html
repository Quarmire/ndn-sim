Named-Data Radio — The Physics That Bites

The honest capacity story of a single-radio broadcast mesh, and what multi-radio / multi-channel and network coding do about it. Every curve is from a parameterised model with half-duplex, interference range beyond decode range, carrier-sense defer-waste, and measured adjacent-channel leakage.

Single-radio multi-hop collapses

Saturated Interest→Data throughput ÷ single-hop channel capacity. Half-duplex + wide interference + carrier-sense + the contending return path put 4 hops near 1/8 — far below the naive 1/hops (dashed).

MRMC lifts it — if channels are spaced

Two radios on two alternating channels let a relay RX+TX at once and stop adjacent hops interfering — the chain pipelines back up (~2×). But on adjacent channels the sideband leakage collapses it right back to single-radio.

Coding repairs the lossy multi-hop path

Transmissions to deliver a K=12 generation over a 2-hop lossy path. In-network recode (F2) repairs each hop locally; end-to-end FEC (F1) re-crosses both; forward has no coding. The gap widens with loss.
