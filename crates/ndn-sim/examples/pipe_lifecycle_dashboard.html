ndn-pipes · #66 · lifecycle under mobility × density

Creation, teardown, recreation — where the setup stops amortizing

The last axis. DCNLA amortizes a heavy setup — a SEEK flood, a per-hop handshake, and one IBSS association — over a long-lived unicast pipe, and rebuilds the whole thing when a segment moves. Named-data radio has no setup: the first Interest broadcasts on the name-group, and a break is repaired by re-expressing it (#70). A model, but every constant is sourced: break rate λ(v) measured in #70, real-engine RTT, thesis IBSS-association, MT7612U rate. And it's generous — DCNLA gets a +10% unicast edge #66 showed it doesn't really have on a chain.

A throughput vs mobility · N=50
The amortization argument holds

At realistic break rates (λ<0.4/s) a 250 ms setup costs only ~9% availability, so DCNLA's generous +10% edge keeps it near parity on raw Mbps until the extreme corner. Throughput is not where the floors diverge.

B control overhead vs density · v=5 m/s
Where DCNLA actually pays

Every rebuild is a SEEK flood + handshake, so DCNLA's control traffic to sustain a flow grows with λ×N. Named-data's re-express is bounded by the local neighbourhood.
