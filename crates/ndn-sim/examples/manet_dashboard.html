ndn-sim · comparative eval #69

Named-data radio vs IP MANET protocols

Every protocol here runs on the same calibrated PHY/MAC — anchored to real MT7612U 802.11s mesh measurements. So the headline is a warning against the easy claim: throughput-over-hops is a substrate story (single-radio collapse vs MRMC retention), not an L3-protocol story. Named-data's real advantages appear on three other axes — caching, mobility overhead, and combining MRMC with caching. Fairness: stock IP/802.11s can't do MRMC natively, so they get one radio; only research IP-MRMC (WCETT/MIC) gets multiple.

A single flow · throughput vs hops
The substrate decides, not the protocol

Retention ÷ the 1-hop link. Four IP protocols and single-radio NDN sit on one curve; the two MRMC protocols sit on another. The lines barely separate within a family.

B multi-consumer · caching
Routing moves packets; naming holds content

Cost (hop-transmissions) to serve K consumers the same content over 4 hops. IP re-fetches end-to-end every time; NDN relays cache, so later consumers hit a copy ~1 hop away.

C mobility · control overhead
No route object means nothing to repair

Control transmissions/sec vs route-break rate λ (N=20). Reactive floods climb with churn; batman is flat-but-stale; stateless NDN sheds route repair entirely.

D MRMC + caching · combined
Where the two ideas compound

3-hop, 8-consumer repeated content. IP-MRMC wins throughput back but still re-fetches per consumer. ndnpipes matches the throughput and caches — effective score = throughput ÷ serve-cost.
